# startup sector package

Use this pack for startup marketing sites selling software, financial products, or operational platforms where product proof and buyer trust must coexist.
Load with the form package matching the page's job, plus 2-3 refs total chosen for the brief.
Refs teach how to think, never what to build.

## The startup mode (avoid)

- Avoid the oversized efficiency, control, or AI promise paired with an email capture or two generic conversion CTAs; Brex uses a 72px Inter headline, Ramp leads with “Time is money,” and Rippling splits an AI claim from a “Create free account” form.
- Avoid treating white or warm-neutral SaaS surfaces as a complete identity, especially Brex's #FFFFFF/#FCFCFD/#F3F3F7 stack, Deel's #FFFFFF/#FFFBF4/#FAF4EE stack, or Rippling's #F2EEEB field with an orange #FFA81D button.
- Avoid the dark premium developer skin assembled from Retool's #151515 base, #e9ebdf ink, hairlines, rounded prompt boxes, pastel semantic accents, and a cinematic product backdrop.
- Avoid expanding one platform into a rounded card matrix, tabbed workflow proof, alternating UI feature rows, KPI band, testimonial carousel, resource feed, and final conversion panel.
- Avoid polished phone, card, dashboard, or workplace imagery surrounded by floating status cards; Brex uses pristine card-and-iPhone renders, Deel overlays payroll and visa cards on lifestyle photography, and Rippling pairs animated UI with literal dashboards.
- Avoid staging trust as a recognizable grayscale or scrolling logo wall immediately after the hero, then repeating customer counts, quantified savings, review scores, awards, and executive testimonials throughout the page.
- Avoid repeating “Get started,” “Book a demo,” or “Start for free” in the navigation, hero, closing panel, and footer before ending in a directory-scale dark footer with legal, compliance, app-store, and product-link blocks.

## Tells

- A huge AI or efficiency claim above an email form and rounded product-demo frame is the fastest startup-default tell.
- A worker or device render covered by floating mini-dashboard cards signals interchangeable platform breadth.
- A “trusted by” count followed immediately by a moving logo wall turns credibility into a template beat.
- Product tiles, KPI counters, testimonial cards, and a repeated CTA stacked above a mega-footer reveal the standard long-scroll anatomy.

## Register

- Credibility is load-bearing when it is product-faithful: Amie, Attio, Figma, Granola, and incident.io use real interface behavior, data grammar, or playable demonstrations as evidence rather than decorative mockups.
- Customer counts and logos are supporting evidence, while named outcomes, relevant compliance such as Retool's SOC 2 and ISO 27001, and financial disclosures such as Brex's FDIC, FINRA, SIPC, rate, risk, and fee language carry procurement trust.
- The audience expects concise, operationally literate language; excellence can still sound distinctive through Granola's editorial serif, incident.io's serif-and-sans contrast, Cash App's plainspoken financial copy, or Clay's playful metaphor.
- One structural brand move is more credible than ambient novelty: Cash App owns #00E013 as a hero field, Clay concentrates its contraption in the opening, Mercury concentrates motion in a sticky hero, and incident.io reserves #f25533 for the narrative and conversion spine.
- Product breadth should become navigable or demonstrable, as Mercury's labeled banking flows and Attio's technical CRM canvas show, rather than dissolving into a generic six-card feature inventory.
- Excellence proves that startup clarity can range from Amie's quiet 1024px product rail to Cash App's full-viewport color field without surrendering legibility, responsive hierarchy, or evidence.
