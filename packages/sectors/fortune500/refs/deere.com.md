## Lessons (3-5 bullets)
- Treat industrial product photography as responsive content, not merely a crop: Deere supplies distinct aspect ratios and image assets per breakpoint so the machine and operator remain legible.
- Separate the assertive voice from the workmanlike interface: heavy Inter headlines carry campaign energy while Open Sans keeps specifications, labels, and dense navigation readable.
- Let a narrow semantic palette do most of the work: green signals brand and primary action, yellow is reserved for accent, and warm neutrals create hierarchy without competing with equipment imagery.
- Standardize radically different page types on the same shells and primitives: cinematic home heroes, category card rows, breadcrumbs, and searchable product listings all retain common gutters, widths, type, and spacing.
- Build commerce-grade wayfinding into the editorial system: persistent dealer, search, compare, offer, and breadcrumb components turn inspiration into a clear product-selection path.

## Avoid (1-2 bullets)
- Do not copy the extremely wide `4:1` hero without Deere's breakpoint-specific alternate crops; a single responsive image would make subjects tiny or crop away the equipment.
- Do not spread green and yellow across every surface: their clarity depends on white/warm-gray restraint and near-black typography, with yellow functioning as an accent rather than a general background.
