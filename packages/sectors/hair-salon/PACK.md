# hair-salon sector package

Use this pack for full-service hair salons, appointment-led hair studios, and salons specializing in Korean and other Asian hair services and techniques.
It excludes barber shops, beard-grooming businesses, and clipper-first barbershop briefs. Men's cuts, perms, color, treatments, and styling remain in scope when they belong to a hair-salon service system.

## Register

- Load-bearing credibility sits at the decision point: clear service definitions, fixed versus starting or consultation pricing, durations where known, inclusions, length or density adjustments, consultation rules, eligible stylists, and relevant result work.
- Korean and other Asian hair-service expertise needs specific proof. Service vocabulary, a professional's attributable training or work history, category-matched results, and languages actually offered carry more weight than a salon name or a broad claim about Eastern techniques.
- Named stylist profiles should expose role, specialties, experience, service eligibility, and available communication languages. An any-stylist route can reduce friction, but it does not replace enough information for a considered choice.
- Attributed reviews and social channels can support confidence. They cannot substitute for owned service information, current prices, named expertise, and hair work that a client can connect to the service menu.
- Use warm, plain, precise language for services and logistics. Inflated luxury and efficacy claims create risk when the site cannot show who performs the work or substantiate the outcome.
- Excellence spans editorial portraiture, dark monochrome, youthful social energy, warm neighborhood care, and utility-led service depth. Hair-salon credibility has no required palette, type pairing, or luxury register.
- **Operational surface:** keep the service index, service detail, price status, consultation route, stylist choice, FAQ, and key policies on the owned site, then hand booking off with enough context to preserve the choice.
- **Operational consistency:** use one current source for website and booking prices, durations, inclusions, cancellation terms, staff availability, and location details. Contradictory haircut prices, notice periods, old salon names, or branch details undermine otherwise strong utility.
- **Local access:** expose the exact address, floor or suite, branch-specific hours, phone or text route, map, transit or landmark guidance, and parking details that actually apply. Multi-location salons should route to branch facts and the correct booking destination.
- **Photography:** make owned client-result work a primary category signal and organize it with the same cut, color, perm, treatment, or styling vocabulary used in services. Interior and staff-at-work images establish place and atmosphere, while stock campaign models cannot prove local outcomes.
- **Photography breadth:** show the real range of services and clients the salon accepts. If men's and children's services are offered, a women-only portrait treatment misstates the business. Give autoplay reels, marquees, and walkthroughs a reduced-motion fallback.
- **International-student access:** explain local service terms, starting-price uncertainty, likely appointment duration, consultation steps, cancellation rules, and arrival directions in plain language. These details help anyone planning around classes, transit, budget, or unfamiliar booking conventions; international-student status is not an ethnicity or a style preference.
- **Asian-client care:** treat texture, density, chemical history, goals, and maintenance needs as individual consultation inputs. Do not infer language, hair behavior, gender, desired style, or familiarity with a technique from appearance, name, or nationality, and do not treat "Asian hair" as one texture.
- **Language and terminology:** state only the languages the team can actually support, let the client choose, and explain terms such as setting, digital, iron, down, volume magic, or straight perm alongside familiar alternatives. Korean-language support, Korean techniques, Asian clients, and international students are separate facts and should never be collapsed into one audience label.
