# bigtech sector package

Use this pack for corporate, product, research, impact, and careers pages where big-tech authority must feel specific rather than templated.
Load it with the form package matching the page's job, plus 2-3 refs total chosen for the brief.
Refs teach how to think, never what to build.

## The bigtech mode (avoid)

- Avoid the topical announcement hero built as either a split headline-and-looping-demo or a full-screen autoplay carousel with one rounded blue CTA.
- Avoid defaulting to dark navy copy, white cards, pale blue-grey section bands, and corporate blue actions such as Google `#1a73e8`, Meta `#0064E0`, or Microsoft `#0078D4`.
- Avoid the predictable march from hero to three equal cards, image-and-copy split, impact or responsibility module, newsletter or leadership close, and mega-footer.
- Avoid making every media card a softly rounded container with 16:9 imagery, thin grey borders, hover zoom or shadow, and a pill or circular-arrow action.
- Avoid the stock big-tech image mix of polished people using devices, workplace engineers, product UI, lab hardware, gradient abstractions, and documentary impact photography.
- Avoid staging trust as safety, privacy, responsibility, sustainability, leadership, and impact links without concrete proof beyond the corporate mark and institutional story.
- Avoid letting the footer become the strongest information architecture through 31-link, 61-link, or similarly exhaustive product, policy, support, legal, and locale directories.
- Avoid generic careers credibility built from a named employee portrait and first-person story beside equal-width Teams, Job categories, and Locations cards.

## Tells

- A silent product film, mission slogan, and solitary blue pill CTA announce the sector default immediately.
- Three equal photographic or 16:9 cards on a pale field turn products, research, or responsibility into the same interchangeable module.
- White cards, dark navy copy, rounded blue actions, and a compliance-heavy mega-footer create the standard corporate frame.
- Consumer products giving way to AI, safety, impact, and legal navigation is the default narrative arc.

## Register

- Load-bearing trust comes from inspectable product behavior, named research or institutional work, and specific employee or leadership attribution rather than badges, awards, or unsupported headline statistics.
- Safety, privacy, human rights, sustainability, and accessibility matter when they lead to substantive policy or transparency material, while a trio of soft-grey responsibility cards is only decoration.
- The audience expects calm, direct, technically literate language with short task-led actions such as search, shop, learn, download, or read rather than inflated corporate reassurance.
- Strong work can make the product interaction itself the proof, as OpenAI does with a functional composer and Airbnb does with a task-shaped discovery control.
- Excellence can also earn authority through disciplined spectacle, including Apple's single-idea image stages, DeepMind's grid-aligned scientific media, Netflix's cinematic careers narrative, and NVIDIA's action-focused state color.
- Brand color is most credible when it signals action, selection, data, or live state, not when it floods the interface.

## Range map

- airbnb.com - This is the task-structured discovery pole, with adaptive grids and saturated color reserved for the final action.
- apple.com - This is the art-directed product-minimalism pole, where one image and sparse copy carry each stage.
- deepmind.google - This is the scientific-spectacle pole, combining scholarly structure, responsive scale, and theme-aware motion media.
- jobs.netflix.com - This is the entertainment-led employer-brand pole, using cinematic chapters without losing the concrete job action.
- nvidia.com - This is the high-performance media pole, with strict grid discipline and `#76b900` reserved for action and live state.
- openai.com - This is the product-as-proof editorial pole, led by a real composer inside a restrained black-and-white system.
- spotify.design - This is the category-coded editorial pole, where controlled color themes and motion organize stories.
- stripe.com - This is the technical-product warmth pole, pairing dense grid infrastructure with localized spectral color and code-native typography.
