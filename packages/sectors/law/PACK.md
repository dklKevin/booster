# law sector package

Use this pack for law-firm, legal-practice, matter, insight, and recruiting pages where institutional authority must coexist with usable editorial content.
Load with the form package matching the page's job, plus two or three references total chosen for the brief.
Refs teach how to think, never what to build.

## The law mode (avoid)

- Avoid the prestige carousel as a default hero, whether it is Cravath's centered deal ticker, Kirkland's 49/50 split screen, Latham's 90vh editorial slider, or Sullivan & Cromwell's five-slide transaction billboard.
- Avoid treating navy, black, white, and firm red as automatic credibility, especially combinations like #011230 and #15406A, #1C1C1C and #2354E8, or #0e0e0e and #e82011.
- Avoid reflexively pairing a large heritage serif with tracked uppercase sans-serif labels, as seen in Bembo with Franklin Gothic, Playfair Display with Rubik, and FFMartinaPlantijnLight with Graphik and FFRealText.
- Avoid the standard scroll anatomy of hero carousel, news or deal carousel, rankings band, image-card grid, and exhaustive office footer.
- Avoid generic prestige imagery such as glass towers, city skylines, Supreme Court columns, server rooms, infrastructure, abstract technology, and darkened deal photography chosen mainly to hold white overlay text.
- Avoid staging trust as an undifferentiated stream of multibillion-dollar values, league tables, Chambers rankings, publisher awards, and dated “Firm Advises” headlines.
- Avoid repetitive utility CTAs such as “Read More,” “View All,” “More News,” and “Explore” set in uppercase with arrows, red or blue accents, and little explanation of the destination's value.
- Avoid making global scale a footer spectacle through long office inventories, practice directories, social links, disclaimers, and attorney-advertising notices on dark or institutional-gray fields.

## Tells

- A giant serif transaction value sits inside a rotating, darkened blue billboard.
- Tracked uppercase eyebrows and arrowed “Read More” links frame every editorial module.
- Glass architecture, skylines, or abstract technology carry white prestige copy over a gradient.
- Deals, rankings, awards, and office counts accumulate as proof without helping a visitor choose or act.

## Register

- Load-bearing trust comes from specific matters, outcomes, current analysis, named clients where permitted, and attributable third-party recognition; generic badges, praise, and decorative prestige imagery are secondary.
- The audience expects precision, restraint, readable long-form material, clear authorship or contact paths, and confidence without consumer-style ratings or conversion pressure.
- Institutional history and global reach can support credibility, but they should not displace the page's practical job or become the dominant hero and footer narrative.
- Excellence can feel expressive without losing rigor: Cooley uses editorial scale, Fenwick makes case proof visual, Freshfields repeats one construction motif, and Quinn Emanuel turns litigation confidence into a direct sequence from statement to victories.
- Strong systems assign accents distinct roles and preserve sober reading grounds, as shown by Goodwin's orange actions and blue structure, Quinn Emanuel's cyan structure and lime state cues, and Freshfields' blue interaction states on black and warm off-white.
- Credibility survives beyond the traditional serif template, because Fenwick, Orrick, and Quinn Emanuel build hierarchy with disciplined sans families while WSGR uses a precise sans display face with a readable serif body.

## Range map

cooley.com - It represents editorial monumentality organized by a strict grid, role-based color themes, and one recurring motion language.
fenwick.com - It represents technology-sector confidence through an expanded grotesk, asymmetric proof tiles, and a single assertive blue.
freshfields.com - It represents warm, fluid modernism anchored by black typography, pastel-blue interaction, and a repeatable open-corner frame.
goodwinlaw.com - It represents service discovery and live expertise in a measured editorial system with humane serif contrast and restrained branded interaction.
orrick.com - It represents immersive brand theatre separated cleanly from compact, predictable legal reading and author structure.
quinnemanuel.com - It represents assertive litigation confidence through cinematic imagery, immediate victories, and a disciplined cyan-and-lime role split.
wsgr.com - It represents editorial precision through sans-display and serif-body contrast, compact color fields, and controlled directional geometry.
