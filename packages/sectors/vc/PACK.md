# vc sector package

Use this pack for venture-capital firm homepages, portfolio directories, thesis pages, and editorial or company-story surfaces.
Load with the form package matching the page's job, plus 2-3 refs total chosen for the brief.
Refs teach how to think, never what to build.

## The vc mode (avoid)

- Avoid making a near- or full-viewport prestige hero the whole proposition, whether it is a cinematic autoplay reel, an auto-advancing editorial carousel, or an oversized partnership claim with proof pushed to a side rail.
- Avoid the inherited institutional palette of monochrome or warm off-white fields such as `#f6f6f6`, `#f3f4ef`, and `#fbf7f0`, relieved only by dark ink, muted pastels, metallic gold, or a legacy green.
- Avoid treating serif authority, light-weight oversized sans, condensed display faces, or outlined SVG slogans as a substitute for a specific investment point of view.
- Avoid the familiar sequence of minimal or sticky navigation, one grand statement, a portfolio or editorial showcase, newsletter capture, and a compact but institutionally dense footer.
- Avoid borrowing credibility from a wall of famous company names, IPO headlines, founder portraits, or generalized scale metrics such as AUM, office count, portfolio count, and firm age without connecting the evidence to a thesis.
- Avoid company-brand imagery as ambient decoration: cover-cropped 16:9 slides, dark bottom gradients, glass “Read More” pills, and mixed portraits, screenshots, or branded graphics have become standard portfolio theater.
- Avoid staging trust through absence and prestige alone, where badges and performance evidence disappear while taxonomy-heavy navigation, partner bylines, disclosures, and restrained editorial CTAs imply authority.

## Tells

- A full-screen film or carousel makes the firm feel like a cultural brand before it says what the firm believes.
- A giant mission line in acres of whitespace is followed by famous portfolio names that do the evidentiary work.
- Dark gradients, bottom-left white copy, and a translucent “Read More” pill turn every company story into the same prestige slide.
- A permanent metrics rail or sweeping practice taxonomy substitutes institutional scale for an argued point of view.

## Register

- Load-bearing trust comes from named companies, specific founder or company stories, a legible investment thesis, current publishing, and dated scale facts; badges, awards, and accreditations are not required by either the mode or the excellence set.
- The audience expects confident, compressed language and low-pressure actions, but the excellence references show that restraint can still be plainspoken, technical, playful, or editorial rather than generically august.
- Portfolio proof should explain judgment: Contrary makes it architectural, First Round ties companies to founding insights, Lux embeds frontier technology in its thesis, and USV places current writing beside stated beliefs.
- Motion earns credibility only when it explains the thesis or navigation, as at Lux, Pace, Seven Seven Six, and Founders Fund; an autoplay reel or long scroll without that semantic job is decoration.
- Operational clarity matters after expression: consistent directories, practical grids and filters, readable measures, reduced-motion handling, and coherent token roles prove that a distinctive homepage can still support diligence.
- **Operational surface:** keep the thesis, a portfolio with company links, team, writing, and a contact path on the owned site. USV gives the thesis a column; First Round ties each company to a founding insight; Contrary puts portfolio in the same grid as research.
- **Operational consistency:** portfolio names and URLs on the homepage must match the companies directory. A logo wall that does not resolve to a company page is decoration.
- **Photography:** founder portraits, company stories, and thesis media with a job. Generic skyline decks and unlinked logo gardens are the mode.
