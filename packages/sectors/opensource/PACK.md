# opensource sector package

Use this pack for open-source project, foundation, framework, runtime, and ecosystem websites that must earn both technical and community trust.
Load with the form package matching the page's job, plus 2-3 refs total chosen for the brief.
Refs teach how to think, never what to build.

## The opensource mode (avoid)

- Avoid the familiar split hero that pairs a mission or runtime slogan with a project-logo mosaic, conference photo, geometric collage, or code panel inside a roughly 1200px container.
- Avoid institutional blue, purple, or green branding applied mechanically across pale gray bands, especially Apache's `#28265E` and `#7C297D`, Linux Foundation's `#003778` and `#5B1DE7`, or Node.js's `#417e38` over `#f6f7f9`.
- Avoid stacked pathway modules that cycle through logo grids, impact-stat rails, membership cards, events, news, newsletters, and generic “Learn More” actions.
- Avoid making ecosystem branding the dominant imagery through six-column square logo tiles, 24-logo grids, sponsor strips, and foundation marks.
- Avoid treating oversized project, release, contributor, member, training, or standards counts as the primary proof before visitors encounter the work itself.
- Avoid generic developer credibility theater in which a dark syntax-highlighted demo, download CTA, release badge, and dense governance footer substitute for a distinctive product argument.
- Avoid repeating uppercase pill CTAs across every section, whether Apache purple `#7C297D`, Linux Foundation pink `#F0A`, or Python yellow `#ffd343`.
- Avoid the heavy institutional close that stacks donation or membership bands, dense navigation groups, legal copy, social links, and foundation ownership into one oversized footer.

## Tells

- A mission-led split hero beside drifting project logos or masked conference photography signals the sector default immediately.
- A giant multi-column strip of ecosystem counts followed by member, project, or sponsor logos reads as borrowed legitimacy.
- A code-demo hero followed by Download, Docs, Jobs, News, Events, and foundation modules reads like a project portal rather than a designed argument.
- Repeated pale bands, three-up cards, and “Learn More” pills make every pathway feel interchangeable.

## Register

- Load-bearing trust comes from inspectable proof: runnable commands, real code, current releases, product UI, project output, dated activity, governance, security, and licensing.
- Sponsor marks, nonprofit status, member totals, and donation prompts are supporting context; they cannot replace evidence that the software works and is maintained.
- The audience expects direct, technically literate language, with code and setup treated as usable content rather than decorative syntax.
- Excellence can be cinematic without becoming corporate: Blender lets community artwork persuade, Ghost lets product footage carry proof, and Astro and Vite place a first successful command near the proposition.
- Distinctiveness is strongest when the project's own abstraction becomes the visual system, as Tailwind's utility labels, Laravel's drafting lines, htmx's code-shaped wordmark, and Svelte's machine demonstrate.
- Credibility survives personality when roles stay disciplined, as Gleam's mascot and pink community cues show beside real code and a consistent technical rail.
