## Lessons (3-5 bullets)
- Keep global retail chrome operational and consistent, then let merchandising change register: the same 1246px shell, 20px gutters and fixed breakpoints support campaign imagery, category navigation and PDP detail without forcing one visual template onto all three.
- Use typography to separate selling modes: compact 14px Open Sans carries search, filters, links and purchasing, while sparingly used 24-36px ruled serif headings mark editorial stories and collections.
- Scale product discovery by intent: broad homepage cards use a 2/3/4-column progression, while compact category families can reach six columns at 1023px, reserving more area for items that need evaluation.
- Treat accessibility states as brand tokens: the repeated 4px yellow focus halo is conspicuous across blue, green, white and gray surfaces and is more dependable than subtle border changes.
- Give information-dense pages a quiet substrate: `#EEEEEF` separates white product/content surfaces while dark blue header and footer bands keep wayfinding strongly anchored.

## Avoid (1-2 bullets)
- Copying the Times New Roman rule-framed heading everywhere would turn a restrained catalog cue into decoration and weaken the clear Open Sans commerce hierarchy.
- Reusing the six-column category density for product cards or PDP content would remove the image and copy space that the source deliberately preserves with lower column counts and 1206px caps.
