## Lessons (3-5 bullets)
- Assign color by retail job: blue carries navigation and ordinary action, red isolates price and savings, green signals availability or purchase, and quiet warm greys hold the dense supporting interface.
- Let product imagery dominate the decision canvas while reserving a stable 35-40% rail for title, price, financing, availability, and conversion; the extracted desktop product grid formalizes that split as 3/5 plus 2/5.
- Use condensed type for high-information commerce moments such as product titles and price banners, while the regular family handles browsing and explanatory copy; this creates hierarchy without introducing an unrelated display face.
- Turn service into visible interface: Sweetwater repeats a real advisor portrait, name, phone path, and product-specific help at several decision points, making expertise part of the shopping system rather than a generic trust claim.
- Scale density by template: the homepage uses large image tiles and 16px gaps, category cards compress names to 14-15px and prices to 25px, and the product page expands the price to 44px while preserving the same color and type roles.

## Avoid (1-2 bullets)
- Copying the site's information density without its strict role-based color, type, and template hierarchy would turn the many promotions, financing details, badges, and support paths into visual noise.
- Treating the Sales Engineer pattern as a decorative headshot would miss its value; it works because the same identifiable expert is connected to direct help throughout the purchase journey.
