# Interface package

For product and tool UI: apps, dashboards, anything with controls.
Load with the installed DESIGN.md plus files from `refs/` chosen for the brief. Never load the whole refs folder.
Refs teach how to think, never what to build.

## Range map

- `refs/teenage-engineering.md`: industrial datasheet; strict grids and a deliberately narrow token set create precision.
- `refs/ghostty.md`: sparse terminal utility; one literal product artifact can carry identity, demonstration, and atmosphere.
- `refs/raycast.md`: cinematic dark launcher; polished spectacle stays credible when it reproduces real product behavior.
- `refs/warp.md`: operational software theater; staged working states make a large product simulation feel purposeful.
- `refs/flighty.md`: guided app journey; scroll-linked state changes turn interface screens into a coherent narrative.
- `refs/family.md`: playful consumer tool; colorful characters remain legible inside a quiet, disciplined shell.
- `refs/posthog.md`: maximal product desktop; a broad product graph can sustain a deep interface metaphor across routes.
- `refs/panic.md`: eclectic product carnival; repeated proportions and grouping can unify strongly independent product voices.

## Lessons (cross-site)

- Neutral chrome lets product content, state, and route identity carry color without making the whole system noisy.
- Literal product states teach faster and feel more credible than generic illustrations or decorative screenshots.
- Multi-route coherence comes from a repeated shell, grid, type roles, and spacing steps while a small set of local variables supplies identity.
- Geometry should match the product's character, but a short radius and spacing vocabulary keeps both strict and friendly interfaces coherent.
- Display scale can be expressive while interface and reading text stay in a compact, dependable hierarchy with restrained weight changes.
- Motion earns its place when it explains state, sequence, or interaction, uses a small timing vocabulary, and yields under reduced-motion preferences.

## Avoid (cross-site)

- Do not borrow a terminal, desktop, phone, or oversized software-window metaphor unless the subject supplies enough real behavior to sustain it.
- Do not spread product-local, illustration, or semantic accents into general chrome; their force depends on a restrained surrounding field.
- Do not carry viewport-scaled display type or sparse hero spacing into dense controls and prose without clamps, contrast checks, and separate reading measures.
